The main point is that the reported comparisons have distinct, recorded setups. The activity comparison on slide 16 uses 48 homologs and the SfaB reference, with one Mg per model and 50 Boltz-2 predictions per enzyme. The mutation examples on slides 20 and 21 use a separate two-Mg campaign and freshly generated matched WT controls. These scores must be interpreted within their respective setups.

Step 1 models the pre-activation protein–ATP–adipate complex. It does not contain a preformed acyl intermediate. No pocket or reaction-distance restraint is supplied to the step-1 panel. Its affinity property requests a separate affinity prediction and is distinct from a spatial binding-site constraint.

The charge audit uncovered a concrete preprocessing difference. Every panel YAML declares dianionic adipate, but all 49 saved processed ligand graphs contain neutral adipic acid. The installed Boltz parser calls its standardization routine before constructing an affinity-requested SMILES ligand. This is evidence of a processed-state difference; it is not a controlled sensitivity experiment or a claim that the difference explains any observed rank. The mutation inputs omit the affinity property and specify adipate charge -2. This slide labels that mutant charge as an input charge because the complete remote processed-ligand set was not independently audited here.

The mutant campaign chemistry audit explicitly records the cached CCD ATP graph as formal charge 0, with the cached molecule hash. It must not be described as ATP4-. Historic panel inputs also request CCD ATP, but the exact historical CCD-cache identity is not independently pinned for every run. The slide preserves this uncertainty rather than assuming a historical ATP charge from current chemistry alone.

Step 2 is a separate cofold, not a trajectory continued from step 1. It contains adipyl-AMP (-2), monoprotonated hexamethylenediamine (+1), and one Mg. HMDA has a neutral NH2 end and a protonated NH3+ end. The neutral N is identified from the saved graph's formal charges and bond topology, not by choosing whichever N lies closest to the carbonyl. A forced pocket constraint requests HMDA proximity to a declared residue set with max_distance 10 Angstrom. The constraint concerns pocket localization, not a forced N–C attack distance. Contact lists are preserved in sources.json. There is no modeled proton-transfer trajectory.

For mechanism-specific activation geometry, AMP + pyrophosphate in the native ATP reaction supports the P-alpha/O3A coordinate; ADP + phosphate supports P-gamma/O3B. DdaG and PuuA illustrate these different coordinates. This is an evidence-based choice of the coordinate to inspect; the geometric measurement does not establish that the non-native target reaction occurs. The discovery coordinate audit records one- or two-Mg inputs per candidate. Native ATP products do not by themselves establish metal stoichiometry.

The activity-panel launch scripts explicitly request 50 diffusion samples without an explicit seed override. No undocumented recycling or sampling-step values are inferred for those historical runs. The mutant first-pass launcher specifies seed 20261019, three recycling steps, 200 sampling steps and 50 samples. WT and mutant have exact query-matched, sequence-specific MSAs. The numerical mutation angles in the figure match the saved variant-metrics table for AS9 G211V and AS23 P318R.

The study uses one protein chain in these inputs. This does not validate a functional biological assembly. Fifty predictions are technical model samples, not biological replicates, an MD trajectory, or equilibrium populations. Geometry alone does not measure a catalytic barrier or experimental turnover.

A defensible sensitivity test would hold sequence, MSA, ligand states, restraints, predictor settings and sample count fixed, vary one assumption at a time (for example one versus two Mg), and compare score and rank stability across independent repeats. Charge and restraint changes deserve the same treatment. This audit does not launch those calculations or present their outcomes.
