----------------------------------------------------------------------
PARAGRAPH
----------------------------------------------------------------------

After the cofolded ensembles are obtained, the next step is to identify which
structural proxies, if any, correlate with experimental measurements. The proxy
library is system-free: a short reaction-specific resolver assigns catalytic
roles in each pose -- nucleophile, electrophile, leaving group, charge centre,
and the ideal attack angle for that chemistry -- and every proxy is then computed
from those roles alone over the superposed ensemble. Eleven proxies span six
mechanistic determinants: ground-state binding (nucleophile-electrophile
approach distance, nucleophile burial), reactive conformation (distance-gated
near-attack fraction, in-line attack-angle deviation, and alignment of the
approach vector with the electrophile's local plane), transition-state
electrostatics (contacts to the atom accumulating negative charge), nucleophile
activation (distance to the nearest candidate base), leaving-group stabilisation
(metal contacts along the breaking bond), and dynamic preorganisation
(positional fluctuation of the reactive pair and the stability of its approach
across the ensemble). Angles are stored as deviation from the mechanism's ideal
rather than as raw angles, so one direction of merit holds across mechanism
classes; the preorganisation proxies are deliberately gate-free, because gated
proxies are undefined for exactly the poses a true negative produces. A seventh
determinant -- the integrated barrier -- is left visibly unfilled, since no
ensemble geometry estimates one. Experimental activity is then used to select,
revise, or reject these hypotheses rather than merely to validate a final
ranking, under three gates: the sign must match the direction declared before
labels were seen; the correlation must survive partialling out model confidence,
which alone can predict activity without reflecting catalysis; and the winner
must clear a null built by permuting labels, rescoring the whole panel, and
keeping the maximum -- the same selection the analyst performs, whose upward bias
grows with panel size and shrinks with the number of actives. In the synthetase
study this procedure retained a distance-gated in-line attack angle as a coarse
enrichment signal and exposed confidence, metal-geometry, and substrate-burial
effects as misleading. In the hydrolase study it rejected static catalytic
geometry as an exclusion criterion. The preliminary results below provide the
quantitative evidence for both decisions.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTES -- two open decisions before this goes in
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. The synthetase hedge. "Coarse enrichment signal" is the right strength, but
   the sentence can be misread as a promotion. On that panel the pre-registered
   test gave p = 0.0016 while charging the full 142-descriptor battery penalty
   gave p = 0.242 (selection null 95th percentile 0.982, only 3 positives). If
   the text asserts the proxy *passed* the selection null, it overstates.
   Defensible alternative: "...retained a distance-gated in-line attack angle as
   a coarse enrichment signal, which the panel's three actives cannot adjudicate
   further."

2. The hydrolase rejection is stronger than a null correlation. On the discovery
   panel the near-attack gate was degenerate -- distance saturated (gated
   fraction 0.86-1.00) and the resulting ranking was rank-unstable to a 5-degree
   threshold shift (Kendall tau <= 0.35, not significant). That is a cleaner
   basis for rejection than "did not correlate" and is worth one clause.

                                   approach_consistency (-),
                                   distance_stability (-)        [gate-free]
integrated_barrier                 UNFILLED by design

Model confidence (e.g. mean complex pLDDT) is a confound covariate partialled
out in screen(), not a proxy. Excluded by design: metal/cofactor coordination
geometry, cofactor-moiety burial, anything addressed by atom name, residue-
specific pKa or named catalytic residues, and composite/fused scores.
